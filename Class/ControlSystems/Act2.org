** Lag Compensator
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       14c8b95f-9b4c-4bd9-874d-529aecfdf7c2
:END:
- Transfer Function:
  \[
  C_{\text{lag}}(s) = \frac{44s + 44}{s + 0.01}
  \]

- Observations:
  - The motor gradually accelerates to the desired speed.
  - Settles without overshooting the setpoint.
  - Control effort is moderate and consistent.

- Explanation:
  - Lag compensation increases low-frequency gain, which improves steady-state accuracy.
  - It does not respond aggressively to rapid changes, hence the slower response.
  - Ideal when smoothness and low overshoot are more important than speed.

** Lead Compensator
- Transfer Function:
  \[
  C_{\text{lead}}(s) = \frac{160000s + 5.6 \times 10^6}{s + 1035}
  \]
- This controller is more aggressive and reactive than lag.

+ Observations:
  - Fast rise time — motor accelerates quickly.
  - Noticeable overshoot before stabilizing.
  - May demand high input voltage initially.

+ Explanation:
  - Lead compensators boost the high-frequency response.
  - They add phase lead which helps speed up response and reduce delay.
  - But this can result in sharp inputs and overshoot if not tuned carefully.


** Comparison of Lag vs Lead
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       907b82cb-d2b1-43af-b1b9-c7fd38ad5547
:END:
+ Summary:
  - Lag = slow, smooth, stable.
  - Lead = fast, overshoots, high control energy.

+ What I Learned:
  - Trade-off between speed and stability.
  - Choosing a compensator depends on system goals:
    + Lag for smooth regulation.
    + Lead for speed-critical systems.

** Additional Test: Input Voltage Saturation
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       0b89f154-e566-4125-b089-3b6a2a3046b4
:END:
+ Setup:
  - Added a `Saturation` block between the controller and the motor input.

+ Result:
  - Response with both controllers became slower.
  - Lead controller was more affected — hit saturation early and lost edge.
  - Lag controller behaved similarly but was less aggressive to begin with.

+ Explanation:
  - Actuator limits restrict the available control force.
  - Controllers must be designed knowing these limits to avoid integrator wind-up or long rise times.

** Additional Test: Sensor Feedback Delay
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       81f9cf52-cbae-448f-9fc2-8335b86f85d5
:END:
+ Setup:
  - Inserted a `Transport Delay` block with 0.01s delay in the speed feedback path.

+ Result:
  - Lead controller became unstable at times — overshoot increased significantly.
  - Lag controller showed more tolerance to delay but still lost performance.
  - Overall response became slower and less predictable.

+ Explanation:
  - Delay introduces phase lag in the feedback loop.
