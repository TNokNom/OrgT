* Maxwell Equations
+ Maxwell Equations describe how electric fields and magnetic fields
  originate and interact
+ In electrostatics charges are stationary

* Charge Distribution And Density
+ Charge distribution refers to how electric charge is spread within a region of space
  it can be characterized in three primary ways
+ Its important to note that current density is typically zero in electrostatics since charges are stationary

** Volume Charge Density
+ \(\rho\) Represents the amount of charge per unit of volume within a three dimensional region
 * This density is used when charges are distributed throughout a volume, such as in charged dielectric
   or plasma
** Surface Charge Density
+ \(\sigma\) Represents the charge per unit area on a surface, expressed as \(\frac{C}{m^{2}\)
  * Describes charges confined to a surface , such as on a conductor's surface or a charged sheet.
** Line Charge Density
+ \(\lambda\) Represents the charge unit per length along a line , expressed as \(\frac{C}{m}\)
  * Describes charges distributed along a wire or filament

** Current Density
\(J\) Represents the flow of electric charge per unit area through a surface, it is a vector field
  that indicates both magnitude and direction of current flow at each point in space
  * Describes how charges move within materials and across boundaries
*** Convection Current
+ Aries from the bulk movement of free charges, such as electrons drifting in a wire
  * Directly related to the velocity of charge carries and their density
*** Conduction Current
+ Results from the movement of charges due to an electric field within a conductive material
   * described by Ohm's law as \(J=\sigma E\) where \(\sigma\) is the conductivity and E is the electric field
* Coulomb's Law And Electric Force
+ Coulomb's law quantifies the electric force between two point charges
   * It states that the electric field (*E*) at point *P* is due to a signle charge *q* located at *Q* is given by
     \(E = (\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_{o}}) * (\frac{q}{r^{2}} * r^{^})\)
    * Where (*r*) is the distance between *Q* and *P*, and (*r^*) is the unit vector from (*Q*) to (*P*) and \(\epsilon_{o}\)
       is the permittivity of free space
+ The electric force (*F*) on a test charge \(q_{o}\) placed at (*P*) is
  + \(Q = \int \rho_{v} dV\)-> total charges in a volume
  -> \( F = q_{o} \cdot E\)
* Electric Field Due to Multiple Charges And Distributions
 + The electric field at a point resulting from multiple charges is obtained by vector superposition
   * Adding the individual fields due to each charge
  -> \(E_{total} =\sum E_{i}\)
 + For continuous charge distributions the electric field is calculated by
     integrating over entire charge distribution
** Volume Charge
+ \(E = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_{o}} \int \frac{\rho(r^{'})}{(r - r^{'})^{2}} dV\)
** Surface Charge
 + \(E = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_{o}} \int \frac{\sigma}{(r - r^{'})^{2}} dA\)
** Line Charge
 + \(E = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_{o}} \int \frac{\lambda}{(r - r^{'})^{2}} dl\)

* Electric Flux Density
+ (*D*) represents ow much electric flux passed trhough a given area considering the medium's properties
  * Its related to the electric field through this formula
    -> \(D = \epsilon E \)
    -> \(\Delta \cdot D = \rho_{free} \)
