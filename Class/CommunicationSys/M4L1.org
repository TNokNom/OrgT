* 1. Theory of QPSK

** 1.A Derivation of Equation (22.2)

The QPSK passband signal is:
\[
s_i(t) = \sqrt{\frac{2E_s}{T_s}} \cos(\omega_c t - \phi_i)
\]

The orthonormal basis functions are:
\[
\psi_1(t)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{T_s}}\cos(\omega_c t),
\quad
\psi_2(t)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{T_s}}\sin(\omega_c t)
\]

The goal is to show:
\[
s_i(t) = \sqrt{E_s}\cos(\phi_i)\psi_1(t) + \sqrt{E_s}\sin(\phi_i)\psi_2(t)
\]

*** 1.A1 Direct expansion using cosine identity
Using:
\[
\cos(A-B)=\cos A\cos B + \sin A\sin B
\]

\[
s_i(t)=\sqrt{\frac{2E_s}{T_s}}
\left[
\cos(\omega_c t)\cos(\phi_i) + \sin(\omega_c t)\sin(\phi_i)
\right]
\]

Group terms:
\[
s_i(t)
=\sqrt{E_s}\cos(\phi_i)\left(\sqrt{\frac{2}{T_s}}\cos(\omega_c t)\right)
+\sqrt{E_s}\sin(\phi_i)\left(\sqrt{\frac{2}{T_s}}\sin(\omega_c t)\right)
\]

Thus:
\[
s_i(t)=\sqrt{E_s}\cos(\phi_i)\psi_1(t)+\sqrt{E_s}\sin(\phi_i)\psi_2(t).
\]

*** 1.A2 Vector-space approach

**** (a) Compute coordinates
The x-coordinate is:
\[
x_i = \int_0^{T_s} s_i(t)\psi_1(t)\,dt = \sqrt{E_s}\cos(\phi_i)
\]

Similarly:
\[
y_i = \int_0^{T_s} s_i(t)\psi_2(t)\,dt = \sqrt{E_s}\sin(\phi_i)
\]

Thus coordinates:
\[
(x_i,y_i)=\left(\sqrt{E_s}\cos\phi_i,\; \sqrt{E_s}\sin\phi_i\right)
\]

**** (b) Reconstruction
A signal expressed in an orthonormal basis is:
\[
s_i(t)=x_i\psi_1(t)+y_i\psi_2(t)
\]
Substitute the coordinates to obtain (22.2).

** 1.E Mary PSK extension

*** 1.E-1 Coordinates for M = 8
General M-PSK phase:
\[
\phi_i=\frac{2\pi(i-1)}{M}
\]

Coordinates:
\[
x_i=\sqrt{E_s}\cos\left(\frac{2\pi(i-1)}{8}\right)
\qquad
y_i=\sqrt{E_s}\sin\left(\frac{2\pi(i-1)}{8}\right)
\]

Thus for i = 1…8, angles are:
0°, 45°, 90°, 135°, 180°, 225°, 270°, 315°.

The eight points form a circle of radius √E_s.

*** 1.E-2 Relationship between E_s and E_b
Each 8-PSK symbol carries:
\[
\log_2 8 = 3 \text{ bits}
\]

Thus:
\[
E_b = \frac{E_s}{3}
\]

** 2.C Bandwidth comparison of xt, yt, sit

x(t) and y(t) are baseband BPSK signals with identical bandwidth.  
si(t) = x(t)ψ₁(t) + y(t)ψ₂(t) is a linear combination of *orthogonal* carriers at the same frequency.

Because ψ₁ and ψ₂ differ only by a 90° phase shift, they do not widen the spectrum;  
si(t) retains the same bandwidth as each component.

* 4. SNR, AWGN, and Phase Error Effects

** 4.A Compute noise variance for Eb/N0 = 13 dB

1. Convert Eb/N0 to linear:
\[
\text{Eb/N0}_{lin} = 10^{13/10}
\]
\[
= 19.9526
\]

2. Given Eb = 16:
\[
N_0 = \frac{E_b}{Eb/N0} = \frac{16}{19.9526}=0.802
\]

3. Noise variance (per sample):
\[
\sigma^2 = \frac{N_0}{2\,t_{\text{step}}}
= \frac{0.802}{2 \times (1/256)}
= 0.802 \times 128
= 102.656
\]

#+BEGIN_SRC matlab
clear
Nerrs = 50;
EbN0dB_vector=[0 1 3 5 7];
Eb = 16; t_step = 1/256;

for n=1:length(EbN0dB_vector)

    EbN0dB = EbN0dB_vector(n);
    EbN0   = 10^(EbN0dB/10);      % Convert dB to linear
    N0     = Eb/EbN0;
    v_n    = N0/(2*t_step);       % Noise variance expression

    sim('QPSK_BER')
    BER_vector(n) = BER(1);

    BER_theory(n)=0.5*erfc(sqrt(EbN0));
end

figure
semilogy(EbN0dB_vector, BER_vector)
hold on
semilogy(EbN0dB_vector, BER_theory,'r')
legend('Simulated','Theory');grid on; hold off
#+END_SRC
