* Chapter 4.1{**The Ideal Diode**}
- The Ideal Diode is a two-terminal device
- Allows for current flow in one direction (_Anode to Cathode_)
**  Characteristics
- Forward Bias Condition:
  - Zero Forward Voltage Drop: When forward-biased, an ideal diode has no voltage drop across it, meaning it conducts current perfectly without resistance.

- Reverse Bias Condition:
  - Infinite Reverse Resistance: When reverse-biased, an ideal diode does not conduct any current at all, effectively having infinite resistance.
  - Zero Reverse Current: No current flows through the diode when it is reverse-biased.
- Switching Behavior:
  - Instantaneous Switching: An ideal diode switches from conducting to non-conducting and vice versa instantaneously, with no delay.

- Temperature Independence:
  - The characteristics of an ideal diode do not change with temperature. It maintains the same behavior regardless of environmental conditions.

- No Reverse Recovery Time:
  - The ideal diode does not have a reverse recovery time. It can switch off immediately when the reverse bias is applied.

- No Leakage Current:
  - There is no leakage current in the ideal diode when reverse-biased, unlike real diodes which have some leakage current.

- Infinite Breakdown Voltage:
  - An ideal diode can withstand any reverse voltage without breaking down; it exhibits no reverse breakdown behavior.


** Current/Voltage Characteristics
- If a *negative voltage* is applied to the diode no current flows and the
diode behaves similar to an open circuit, this mode is *Reverse Biased*
- If a *positive voltage* is applied then the diode behaves as a short circuit,
this mode is known as *Forward Biased*
** Limting and protection circuits
- Limiter circuits use diodes to limit voltage excursions
- It can safeguard electrical componets and systems

* Chapter 4.3 {*Modeling The Diode*}
** The Exponential Model
- Explains how the current flowing through a diode changes with the voltage applied to it
- The exponential model provides the most accurate description of diode operation
- Its important to note that when you apply a forward voltage, the current dosen't increase
linearly, it instead increases *exponentially*.
- Meaning small increases in voltage can lead to large increases in current
(*outside the breakdown region*)
** Constant Voltage Drop Model
- Of the two models its the most practical
- Assumes that when a diode is forward-biased, it has a constant voltage drop across it
- This voltage drop is .7v  for silicon
** Operation In The Reverse Breakdown Region
-
*** Avalanche Breakdown
- Avalanche Breakdown occurs when a large reverse voltage is
applied
- Its essentially a chain reaction due to the fact that as the electric field
from the reverse voltage gets larger it cases electrons to gain energy and knock
loose other electrons causing a huge *surge* in current
**** Zener Diodes
- Zener diodes are used to prevent the events above
- Built to work reliably in the breakdown region
- It does this by allowing current to flow in the reverse direction when the voltage
exceeds a certain value
* Chapter 4.6{*Rectifier Circuits*}
- A diode rectifier uses diodes to change *AC current* into *DC Current*
- Rectifier circuits typically use three things:
** Recitifier Components:
*** Transformer
- Transformers allow for a stepping down of the voltage
*** Power Supply
- Supplies power
*** Filter
- The output from the diodes is not typically "smooth"
- So filters help reduce this fluctuation
- Even though the filter helps a lot it still produces something known as
a *ripple*, and because of this we need a voltage regulator
*** Voltage Regulator
- Voltage regulator help maintain a constant output voltage
- It does this by adjusting the resistance in the circuit to compensate for the changes, ensuring a stable voltage

** Half Wave Rectifier:
- The half-wave rectifier only uses one half of the AC waveform
- Allowing current to pass during only a certain half of the cycle
-
*Formulas*:
- \(PIV = V_s\)
-  \( v_o = 0\)  for \( v_s < v_d \)
- States that that when the input voltage \(V_s\)(*The AC Source*) is less than the diodes forward voltages drop \(V_D\) the output voltage \(V_o\) is zero
-  \( v_o = v_s - V_D \) for \( v_s > V_D \)
** Full Wave Rectifier
- Converts both halves of an AC waveform into a DC signal
- typically has a smoother and more efficient output
