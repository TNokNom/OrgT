* Magnetic Field Principles
1. When a current passes through a wire, it produces a magnetic field in its vicinity
2. A time-changing magnetic field induces a voltage in a coil of wire if it passes through that coil
3. When a current passes through a wire in the presence of a magnetic field, it has
    a force induced on it (*Motor*)
4. When a wire is moving in the presence of a magnetic field, it has a voltage induced in it
+ In a core the flux density is given by
 * \(\phi = \int_{A}{B \cdot dA}\)
  * With dA being the differential area

+ A magnetic field can be generated by a current
  * We know this through *Ampere's law* \(\oint{H \cdot dl} = I_{net}\)
[[file:../../../MacShots/Magnetic_Field_Principles/2025-08-28_18-50-32_screenshot.png]]
 * H is the magnetic field
 * dl is the differential element of length along the path of integration
+ From this we cant derive these two equation
 \(Hl_{c} = Ni\) , \(H = \frac{Ni}{l_{c}\)

+ The relationship between the magnetic field and the resulting magnetic flux density produced within
a material is given by:
 * \(B = \mu H\)
 * With \(H\) being the magnetic field density
 * With \(\mu\) being the magnetic permeability of material and the permeability of free space  \(\mu_{o} = 4\pi \times 10^{-7} \frac{H}{m}\)
 * With \(B\) being the resulting magnetic flux density produced
 * And the units being \(\frac{A}{m}\) ampere-turns per meter
+ Relative permeability is the permeability of any material compared to the permeability of free space,
it's given by \(\mu_{r} = \frac{\mu}{\mu_{o}}\)
+ Voltage induced on a wire is:
   + \( e_{ind} = N \frac{d\phi}{dt}\)
   * eind = voltage induced in wire
   * v = velocity of the wire
   * B = magnetic flux density vector
   * l = length of conductor in the magnetic field

* Magnetic Field
+ In a core like the one above the magnitude of the flux density is given by:
 * \(B = \mu H = \frac{\mu Ni}{l_{c}\)
+ And the total flux in area is given by:
 * \(\phi = \int_{A} B \cdot dA\)
+ So we can come to conclusion that the total flux in the core due  to \(i\) is given by:
  * \(\phi = BA = \frac{\mu NiA}{l_{c}\)
** Points
+ So from the equations and our knowledge of magnetic circuits we know:
  * The current in the coil of wire in a simple magnetic core produces a magnetic flux in it
  * This is similar to voltage in a electric circuit producing a current flow

+ Reluctance formula is:
\(\mathcal{R} = \frac{l_{C}}{\mu A}\)
+ magnetomotive force (*mnf*) of the circuit is equal to the net current flow applied to the core
  * \(\mathcal{F} = Ni\)
  * This is similar to voltage in a typical electric circuit
  * The magnetomotive force. Is measured in ampere-turns(Amps)
+ Image below shows how they relate to normal electrical concepts
 [[file:../../../MacShots/Magnetic_Circuits/2025-08-28_19-25-29_screenshot.png]]
+  or the relationship between voltage and current in an electric circuit we use ohm's law
to describe it, but for magnetic circuits the *magnetomotive force* and flux can be written as:
 * \(\mathscr{F} = \phi \mathscr{R} \)
 * \(\mathscr{F}\) being the magnetomotive force of the circuit
 * \(\phi\) being the flux
 * \(\mathscr{R}\) being the reluctance of the circuit
+ Another thing is the magnetic equivalent of electrical conductance, known as *Permeance*
  \(\mathscr{P} = \frac{1}{\mathscr{R}}\)
+ The relationship between the magnetomotive force and the flux is expressed as
    \(\phi = \mathscr{FP}\)
