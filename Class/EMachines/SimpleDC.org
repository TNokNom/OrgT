* Linear DC Machine
+ Linear DC machines are the simplest dc machines
  * They operate around the same principles as real generators and motors
  * Are used as example study of machines
+ If \(e_{ind} > V_{b}\) then the circuit is acting as a generator
+ If \(e_{ind} < V_{b}\) then the circuit is acting as a motor
** Equations
 + The power converted from electrical form to mechanical form by a linear motor is given by:
 + \(P_{conv} = \tau_{ind} \cdot \omega\)
 + We can model the behavior of this simple machine with these four equations
   + \(F = i(l \times B)\)
     * F = force on the wire
     * i =  magnitude of current in the wire
     * L = length of wire, with direction of I defined to be  in the direction of current flow
     * B = Magnetic flux density vector
 + \( e_{ind} = (v \times B) \cdot l\)
   * eind = voltage induced in wire
   * v = velocity of the wire
   * B = magnetic flux density vector
   * l = length of conductor in the magnetic field
 + Newtons law for the bar across the tracks
   * \(F_{net} = ma\)

+ For this circuit after the switch is closed a current flows into the bar
  * Using KVL for this machine we get
  * \(v_{b} - iR - e_{ind} = 0\)
  * From this we get \(i = \frac{V_{B}- e_{ind}}{R}\)
+ Since the bar is at rest \(e_{ind} = 0\) so, \(i = \frac{v_{B}}{R}\)
+ As the current flows down through the bar across the tracks
  * We know that a current flowing through a wire in the presence of a magnetic field induces a force on the wire
  * \(F_{ind} = ilB\) to the right
+ This then causes the bar to accelerate to the right, which then causes a voltage to appear across the bar
  * This voltage is given by: \(e_{ind} = \theta \cdot B \cdot l\)
  * And now the current flowing in the bar is: \(i = \frac{V_{B} - e_{ind}}{R}\)
  * It's important to note that as \(e_{ind}\) increases the current decreases
* Summary Of Examples
+ Now we can see that both machines can act as either a motor or generator depending on where the force is applied
+ If the bar hits a region of a weaker magnetic field a *transient will occur*
+ After the transient is over \(e_{ind} = \mathcal{V}_{B}\) VB being the voltage in the battery and if its unloaded
