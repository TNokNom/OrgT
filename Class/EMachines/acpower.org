* REAL, REACTIVE, AND APPARENT POWER
+ Reactive power represents the energy that is first stored and then released in the magnetic field of an inductor, or in the electric field of a capacitor
+ The reactive power of a load is given by:
  * \(P = VIsin\theta\)
  * \(P = S \ cos \theta\)
+ The apparent power (*S*) supplied to a load is defined as the product of the voltage across the load
   and the current through the load.
  * The apparent power of  a load is:
  * \(S = VI\)
  * And its units are volt-amperes
** Additional Equations
+ For,real,reactive,and apparent power their equations are expressed below
  * \(P = I^{2}R\)
  * \(Q = I^{2}X\)
  * \(Q = S sin \theta\)
  * \(S = I^{2}Z\)
  * Here *R* is the resistance
  * *X* is the reactance
  * *Z* is the magnitude of load impedance
* Complex Power
+ First we introduce this equation
  * \(I = \frac{V}{Z} = \frac{V}{|Z|}\)
+ If the impedance angle \(\theta\) of a load is positive the reactive power consumed by load
   will be positive
  * The load is inductive
  * In this case we say that the load is consuming both real power and reactive  power
     from the source
  * The phase angle of the current flowing through the load will lag the phase angle of the
     voltage across the load by \(\theta\)
+ For capacitive loads we have a negative impedance angle

  * The phase angle of the current flowing through the load will lead
     the phase angle of the voltage across the load by \(\theta\)
  * Also, if the impedance angle \(\theta\) is negate
  * The reactive power Q consumed by the load will also be negative
  * So we say that the load is consuming real power from the source
  * And also supplying reactive power to the source
