#+Title: Transformer Notes

* Transformers
+ a Transformer is an electrical device consisting of two or more coils of wire
  used to transfer electrical energy by means of a changing magnetic field
 * Transfer electric power from one circuit to another
 * It does so without a change of frequency
 * Decreases or Increase AC voltage

** Types
+ Current Transformers
  * Produces an alternating current (*AC*) in its secondary which is proportional to the AC current in
    its primary
+ Voltage Transformers
  * also called *potential transformers* are designed to reduce or step down the system under test voltage
  * This is done in order to measure it

** How transformers work
+ When current in the primary coil changes polarity a changing
  magnetic field is produced
 * This changing magnetic failed gets associated with the secondary through the
  soft iron core

** Traits of an ideal transformer
*Zero Leakage Flux:*
- Fluxes produced by the primary and secondary current are
    confined within the core
*Zero Coil Resistance:*
 - No voltage drops on the coils
*Infinite core permeability:*
- Zero core reluctance, meaning negligible current generates the flux
+ The relationship between the input and output voltage, and between the input current and output current is given by:
  * \(\frac{\mathcal{V}_{p} (t)}{\mathcal{V}_{s}(t)} = \frac{N_{p}}{N_{s}} = a\) , \(\frac{\mathcal{I}_{p} (t)}{\mathcal{I}_{s}(t)} =\frac{1}{a}\)
  *  \(\mathcal{V}_{p}(t)\) being the turns on the primary side
  * \(\mathcal{V}_{s}(t)\) being the turns on the secondary side
  * \(a\) is the turn ratio of the transformer
  * \(N_{p}\) is the turns of primary

** Dot Convention
+ The Dot Convention is a method used to identify the polarity of winding
  in a transformer
 * It can help understand the phase relationship between primary
    and secondary voltages or currents.
* Power
+ The power in the primary and secondary winding is given by:
  * \(P_{in} = \mathcal{V}_{p} \ I_{p} \ cos \ \theta_{p}\) & \(P_{out} = \mathcal{V}_{s} \ i_{s} \ cos \ \theta_{s}\)
  * \(\theta\) is the phase angle between the voltage & current
** Impedance
+ For the impedance of a transformer we get
  * \(\mathcal{Z}_{L}' = \frac{\mathcal{V}_{p}}{I_{P}}\)
