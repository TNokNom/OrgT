* Phase
 * Phase in terms of optical waves is the proportion of a periodic waveform that passes
   some reference point after a time \(t\)
 * the symbol omega  \(\omega\) is often used to describe the angular frequency
 * \(\omega = 2\pi f\)
 * The phase of a function like \(\sin(\omega t)\) is the angle -> so in this case \(\omega t\)

 * The period of an angle is always \(2\pi\) & The plot for a function is always on the time axis and not the \(\omega t\) axis
 * When propagating an optical wave its expressed as \(E = E_{o} exp[j(kz \pm \omega t)\) or \(E = E_{o} sin [(kz \pm \omega t)\)
   - with \(k\) being a constant and \(z\) being the progress of the wave in the z direction
* What is Light

 * Light is an electromagnetic wave it has properties of both electric and magnetic fields that vary with time
   -> So essentially we can look at light as a wave
[[file:../../../MacShots/Phase/2025-05-25_03-20-16_screenshot.png]]
 => Here we can see both electric field and the magnetic field in terms of a sinusoidal plane wave
 * There are three characteristics
   1. The wave is a *plane wave* due to the fact that the value of the electric field(*x direction*) and the magnetic
      field (*y direction*), are constant throughout any z-plane
   2. The wave is *transverse* because both electric and magnetic fields are transverse to the direction of propagation
      (*z-direction*)
   3. The wave if *polarised* because the electric and magnetic field exist in only a in single direction
      (*For this wave its polarised in the x direction*) because the electric field only exist in the x direction
* What Is Interference
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       329b8055-a73b-4135-9e26-8b567de62348
:END:
 * In optical circuits two waves are coincident at a given point in space
   the fields of the wave add together (*both electric and magnetic fields*)
 - Two things to note is that if the two waves are in phase something called *constructive interference occurs*
  [[file:../../../MacShots/What_Is_Interference/2025-05-25_14-13-18_screenshot.png]]

 - If they are out of phase they cancel one another
  [[file:../../../MacShots/What_Is_Interference/2025-05-25_14-13-39_screenshot.png]]

 - And if they are out of phase by some set amount the resultant field sum is still a sinusoid of the same frequency
   just shifted in phase and amplitude
[[file:../../../MacShots/What_Is_Interference/2025-05-25_14-13-57_screenshot.png]]
** Assumptions
 * For the interference above two assumptions are made
 * The two waves that are adding are either electric field or magnetic field
 + This implies that they are aligned in terms of Polarisation
 * Also two waves will only intefere if they are coherent
 + coherence describes the phase relationship between two or more waves
  => if two waves are coherent they have a constant phase relationship
 + *Coherence Length* is the distance over which the light retains coherence
   - or the maximum path difference two waves from the same source can experience
     such that interference can still occur
   - It is denoted as => \(L_{c} = \frac{c}{\triangle f \)
   *f* being the change in the spectrum of frequencies contained within the light source
   *C* being the velocity of light
* Waveguide

:ID:       be527663-fc94-4327-bf75-810e1a320059
:END:
[[file:../../../MacShots/Waveguide/2025-06-01_14-10-02_screenshot.png]]
 - This figure shows the concept of waveguide
 - We know that the refractive index of medium 1 (n1) is higher than the refractive index of medium 2 (n2)
    since the angle 2 is greater than the angle 1
 + Because of this we can define the critical angle \(\theta_{c}\) as \(sin \theta_{c} = \frac{n_{2}}{n_{1}}\)
 * For angles of incidence greater than the critical angle defined above
    NO light is transmitted and internal reflection (*TIR*) occurs
 * So essentially what waveguide does, is traps light in between two materials using their refractive index in such a way
   it can't escape the medium
** Reflection Coefficients
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       cfc243a6-e24b-4b0c-bbac-7e1818c6faa1
:END:
 * For a wave that is reflected and transmitted we can describe them using the Fresnel Formula
 + We know the reflected wave will have a complex amplitude \(E_{r}\) at the interface
    related to the complex amplitude \(E_{i}\) of the incident wave by
 +  \(E_{r} = rE_{i}\)
 + r being the complex reflection coefficient
 -> The reflection coefficient is a function of both angle of incidence and the Polarisation of light
 * We know that the electromagnetic and magnetic fields of a wave are always orthogonal (*Separated by 90 degrees*)
 + While both of them are orthogonal to the direction of propagation (*the way in which energy moves through a medium*)
 * *Transverse Electric* (TE) is a condition defined as the condition when the electric field of the waves
    are perpendicular to the plane of incidence.
+ *Transverse Magnetic* (TM) condition occurs when the magnetic fields are perpendicular to the plane of incidence
 [[file:../../../MacShots/Waveguide/2025-06-02_00-42-08_screenshot.png]]
*** Formulas For TE Polarisation and TM Polarisation
 TE => \[
 r_{TE} = \frac{n_{1} cos\theta_{1} - n_{2}cos\theta_{2}}{ n_{1} cos\theta_{1} + n_{2}cos\theta_{2}}
 \]

 TM =>  \[
 r_{TE} = \frac{n_{2} cos\theta_{1} - n_{1}cos\theta_{2}}{ n_{2} cos\theta_{1} + n_{1}cos\theta_{2}}
 \]
