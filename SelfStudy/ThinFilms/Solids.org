* Solids
+ Solids can be classified into the following categories
+ Solids can either be internally crystalline or non-crystalline
  * Those that are crystalline can be subdivided according to one of 14 different geometric arrays or lattices depending on the placement of atoms
** Metallic
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       c0803453-bce3-4dd3-a93c-37198be400b1
:END:
+ Metal atoms release some of their outer electrons
  * They move freely around positively charged metal ions

*** Characteristics
+ Good electrical conductivity
+ Good thermal conductivity
+ Malleable
+ Ductile (Can be drawn into wires)
+ Strong but deformable

*** Examples
+ Iron
+ Copper
+ Aluminum

** Ionic
+ Ionic bonds form when electrons are transferred from one atom to another
  * This creates positive and negative ions that attract each other

*** Characteristics
+ Strong bond
+ High melting point
+ Brittle
+ Conducts electricity when molten or dissolved in water
+ Often forms crystalline structure

*** Examples
+ Sodium Chloride
+ Magnesium Oxide
+ Calcium Fluoride

** Covalent
+ Atoms share electrons to achieve stable electron configurations

*** Characteristics
+ Very strong directional bonds
+ High hardness and strength
+ Poor electrical conductivity
+ Often found in ceramics and semiconductors

*** Examples
+ Diamond
+ Silicon
+ Silicon Carbide

** Van der Waals
+ A weak attraction between molecules caused by a temporary or permanent electric dipole

*** Characteristics
+ Weakest of the four bond types
+ Low melting and boiling points
+ Easily separated layers or molecules
+ Important in polymers and layered materials

*** Examples
+ Solid noble gases
+ Molecular Crystals
+ Graphite Layers
+ Many polymers

* Crystalline Solids
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       db0a75c1-6034-41e8-8d99-24568ab40ac1
:END:
+ Many solid materials already possess an ordered internal crystal structure
  * These structures can be thought of and described as a three-dimensional array of points geometrically and repetitively distributed in space such that each point has identical surroundings
+ There are only 14 ways to arrange points in space having this property
  * These point arrays are known as *Bravais Lattices*

+ These dots don't represent atoms yet
  * But they are locations where atoms could be placed
     In 3D there are 14 unique ways these points can repeat
  * These are called the 14 Bravais lattices
+ If we add an atom to each lattice point we start to get a physical crystal

** Examples
+ FCC means Face-Centered Cubic
+ FCC lattice + Cu atoms = Copper crystal
+ Diamond lattice + Si atoms = Silicon crystal
