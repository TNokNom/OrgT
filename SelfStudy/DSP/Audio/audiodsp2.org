* Sampling
+ Typically the things we want to measure are in analog form
  (*a continuous signal ie. sound pressure, sound*)
  * Usually we find them in the form of voltage signals \(v(t)\)
  * In order to accurately record this data an infinite volume of data must be stored for each
    amplitude and time domains
  * Unless the signal is deterministic then we can represent it using a simple equation
+ Quantization: is the process that approximates a continuous amplitude domain variable as a vale
  from a set of allowed quantum levels
+ Discretization: is the process by which signals that are continuous in time are represented by
  a sequence of numbers describing the amplitude of the signal at instants of time

+ The first stage of sampling process is discretization
  * This takes a function that varies continuously with respect to an independent variable
     and converts it into a discrete sequence
+ Take a voltage \(v(t)\)
  * discretization represents the signal by a sequence of numbers,
     where each number records the value of the function at a particular point int time
  * These time points chosen are regularly spaced and are sampled at a constant "*sampling frequency*"
  * The sampling frequency is \( f_{s} = \frac{1}{\triangle t}\)
    * \(\triangle t\) is the sampling period or sampling interval \(T\)
    * \(v(t)\) is represented as the discrete time sequence \(v_{d}(t)\)

+ The relationship between \(V (t) \)  , \(s(t)\) , \(v_{d}(t)\) is given by:

  * *Discrete Time Sequence (vd(t))*:
   - Represents the original continuous signal \( v(t) \) but at distinct time intervals.

  *  *Discrete Signal (vk)*:
   - Created by multiplying the original signal \( v(t) \) with a sampling waveform \( s(t) \).
   - Contains the sampled values at intervals determined by \( k\Delta t \), where \( k \) is an integer (0, 1, 2, ...).

  *  *Sampling Waveform (s(t))*:
   - Composed of repeated impulses (like little spikes) in time.
   - Each impulse has unit magnitude (height of 1).
   - The time between impulses is the sampling period \( \Delta t \).
  *  *Characteristics of Discrete Signal (vd)*:
   - Mostly zero values for most of the time (only has non-zero values when the sampling occurs).
   - Efficiently stored by recording only non-zero values.

  * *Non-Linear Process*:
   - The process of taking samples is non-linear due to the multiplication of the original signal with the sampling waveform.

** Conclusion:
  - The process of sampling involves taking regular snapshots of a signal at specific times and storing only the significant (non-zero) values, which allows for an efficient representation of the original signal in a discrete format.

+ We are given a continuous signal \(x_{c(T)}\)
  * This is some original analog signal that varies with time
  * It exist for all real values of t
+ Often times when working with signals we cant to see what its value is
   at a given interval or specific instant in time
+ So we use a sampler or a sampling process to achieve this
  * For the example below we use a Continuous to Discrete
  * Similar to a analog to digital converter (*ADC*)
+ How do we reconstruct \(x_{c}\) from \(X[n] \)
  * We can do this when \(x_{c}(t)\) is *bandlimited*
  * Meaning our frequency is 0 beyond certain point
  * \(X_{c}(j\Omega) = \int^{\infty}_{-\infty} x_{c}(t) e^{-j\Omega t} dt\)
+ If \(x_{c}(t)\) is bandlimited
  * Then we can reconstruct \(x_{c}(t)\) from \(X[n]\) as long as the sampling
    is > twice as highest frequency in \(x_{c}(t)\)
  * \(\frac{2\pi}{T} > 2 \ \Omega_{max}\) -> *Nyquist Criterion*
** How do we reconstruct the signal

+ In order to reconstruct the sample we use a *sin interpolator*
  * \(x_{r}(t) = \sum^{\infty}_{n = - \infty} x[n] \frac{sin(\pi(t-nT)/T)}{\pi(t-nT)\T\)
  * \(x_{r}(t) = \sum^{\infty}_{n = - \infty} x[n] g_{t} (t-nT)\)
