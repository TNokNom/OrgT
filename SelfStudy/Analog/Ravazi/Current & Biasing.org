* Basic Current Mirrors Examples

 * Here we have a transistor and we want to know to bias it so it operates as
   a stable current source
 * Assuming its in saturation we're are given this equation
 - \( I_{out} = \frac{1}{2} U_{{n}} C_{ox} \frac{W}{L} (\frac{R_{2}{R_{1} + R_{2}} V_{DD} - V_{TH})^{2} \)
 * This equation allows us to see various PVT dependencies of \(I_{out}\)
 * From this equation we can come to the  conclusion that even if our gate source voltage
   is precisely defined our drain current is not

* Cascade Current Mirrors
 * A cascade device can shield a current source, reducing the voltage variations across it
 * *Channel Length Modulation* is the phenomenon that occurs with changes in \(V_{DS}\)
 + When a voltage \(V_{DS}\) is applied to a *FET* the depletion region expands and subsequently shortens the
    channel length
 + During this the gate voltage has less of an impact on the change in \(I_{DS}\) as the FET approaches
    the *breakdown region*
 + The parameter \(\lambda\) is known as the channel length modulation parameter
 + So we now get the following equation for the drain current in saturation =>
 \[
     I_D = \frac{1}{2} \mu_n C_{ox} \frac{W}{L} (V_{GS} - V_{th})^2 (1 + \lambda V_{DS})
     \]

* Voltage Headroom
+ Voltage Headroom is the amount of voltage left between a circuit's node voltage and a power rail
  that allows transistors to stay in their desired operating region
** Example
+ Given these parameters
 * \(V_{DD} = 1.2V\)
 * MOSFET needs \(V_{DS} > 0.2 V \) to be in saturation
 * If the drain is at 1.1 V and the Source at 1.0 V \(V_{DS} = 1.1 - 1.0 = 0.1V\)
 * There is simply not enough voltage headroom given that to be in saturation
    the \(V_{DS} \geq V_{GS} - V_{TH} \)
