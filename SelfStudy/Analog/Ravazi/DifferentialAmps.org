- A differential amplifier is an amplifier that can is meant to amplify the difference in signal between
  two inputs
** Background
*** Single Ended Signal
- A singled-ended signal is one that is measured with respect to a fixed reference point,
  usually ground
- So given a simple audio signal, the signal would vary based on amplitude, but all measurements would
  be taken relative to ground
*** Differential Signal
- A differential signal is one that is the voltage difference between two points in a circuit
- Each Node oscillates around a common reference point, but in opposite directions
*** Common Mode Level
- Common Mode Level is the average of the voltage level of the two signals
*** Advantages
- Noise Immunity => Differential signaling is great for noise rejection
- It can do this because if some noise were to affect both signals equally
  the difference between them would stay consistent

* In Depth

** Differential Victims And Differential Aggressors
*** Differential Victims
- Differential Victims are signals or circuits experiencing
  interference or degradation
*** Differential Aggressors
- Signals or Circuits that introduce interference on other signals
**** Example
- Noise from a clock line on differential pairs can corrupt data
- The clock acts as the aggressor
- The differential pair is the victim
