** What Is A Differential Amplifier
 * A differential amplifier is one that responds only to the difference between two signals
  applied at its input
 * \(V_{o} = A_{d}(v_{1} - v_{2})\)
  - For Differential amplifiers we should have these five things
1. Infinite input impedance
   - No current leaks out from either of our input terminals
2. Infinite gain for the differential signal
   - The gain \(A_{d}\) should be very high
3. Zero common-mode gain
   - The increase or decrease of v1 and v2 shouldn't show up at the output
4. Zero output impedance
   - The amplifier should act like a perfect voltage source i.e no internal resistance

* Differential Signal
 * A differential signal is one that is the voltage difference between two points in a circuit
 * Each Node oscillates around a common reference point, but in opposite directions

** Common Mode Level
  * Common Mode Level is the average of the voltage level of the two signals
** Advantages
 * Noise Immunity => Differential signaling is great for noise rejection
 * It can do this because if some noise were to affect both signals equally
  the difference between them would stay consistent
