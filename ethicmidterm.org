* ⚖️ Risk Acceptance Criteria – Perception of Risk & Safety
Back: Standards or thresholds used to decide if a risk is tolerable, often based on probability, severity, and societal values.

* What factors influence the Perception of Risk?
Back: Familiarity, control, voluntary vs. involuntary risk, catastrophic potential, and media attention.

* 🧩 Design for Safety Boeing 737 MAX Case
Back: Systemic failure in design (MCAS system), poor pilot communication, lack of redundancy, and organizational pressure overriding safety culture.

* What lessons can engineers learn from this case Boeing 737?
Back: Prioritize safety over deadlines, ensure transparency in design changes, and test thoroughly for all failure modes.


* What type of risk analysis could have prevented the Titan sub disaster?
Back:

+ FMEA (Failure Mode and Effects Analysis)

+ Fault Tree Analysis

+ Independent peer review of materials & pressure testing

+ Adherence to classification standards for deep-sea vehicles

* Front: What was the main ethical failure of the titan sum?
Back: Ignoring expert warnings, lack of certification, and prioritizing innovation over verified safety.

💥 Accident Categories

* 💥 What are the three categories of engineering accidents?
Procedural: Due to poor management or policy.

Engineered: Due to design or material failure.

Systemic: Due to organization-wide or cultural issues.

* Give examples of each type.
+ Procedural: Bhopal Gas disaster (poor safety oversight)

+ Engineered: NASA Challenger explosion (O-ring design)

+ Systemic: Boeing 737 MAX (organizational pressure and design concealment)


* 📣 What is whistleblowing?
Back: When an employee reports unethical or unsafe practices to internal or external authorities.

* What are the types of whistleblowing?

+ Internal: Report within the organization.

+ External: Report to outside authorities or media.

* 📣 What are the four conditions for morally acceptable whistleblowing?

+ Serious harm is likely.

+ Internal reporting was attempted.

+ There’s documented evidence.

+ Whistle blowing can prevent harm.


* 🧑 What are key professional responsibilities of engineers?

+ Protect public safety and welfare.

+ Maintain integrity and honesty.

+ Respect confidentiality and proprietary info.

+ Avoid conflicts of interest.

* What is “Conflict of Interest”?
Back: A situation where personal interest interferes with professional judgment or objectivity.

* What are examples of conflicts of interest?
Back: Financial gain, favoritism, or personal relationships affecting decisions.

* What is competitive bidding?
Process of bidding for contracts ethically, ensuring fairness, transparency, and honesty without deception.


* ⚖ What are the professional rights of engineers?

+ Right to professional recognition and fair compensation.

+ Right to refuse unsafe or unethical work.

+ Right to report unethical conduct without retaliation.

+ Right to participate in decision-making affecting safety.
