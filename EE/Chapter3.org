* Chapter 3.1:
** Semiconductor Theory:
-> Semiconductors are materials whose conductivity lies between copper and insulators like glass

*** Physic Behind Semiconductors:
 -> A silicon atom has four valence electrons (*Valence Electrons being the electrons in the outermost shell*)
 -> _This would cause an element to be unstable_
 -> To counteract this silicon shares it's Valence Electrons with 4 surrounding atoms making it 8 total valence electrons for each atom
  -> This ends up creating an almost crystal like lattice structure
** Types:
*** Single element semiconductors:
-> Germanium
-> Silicon
*** Compound semiconductors:
-> gallium-arsenide

** Chapter 3.2 {*Doped Semiconductor*}
-> In order to use silicon crystal as a semiconductor we have to fix the problem of the lack of free electron holes at certain temperatures

** Doping:
-> Doping involves introducing impurity atoms into the silicon crystal in sufficient numbers to substantially increase the concentration of free electrons or holes
*** N Type:
-> N type materials are created when we *increase the concentration of free electrons*
*** P Type:
-> P type materials are created when we *increase the concentration of holes*
*** Ex:
-> Say we want to increase the concentration of free electrons
  -> We can dope silicon with an element with a valence of 5 (*Phosphorus*) this would cause
    the resulting material to be *n type*

* Chapter 3.4{*The PN Junction*}
-> The pn junction is an example of a p-type silicon structure being brought in contact
   with a n-type silicon structure


* Diode:
** Foward Bias Reigon:
-> a condition in a semiconductor device where a positive voltage is allied to the p-type material and a negative voltage to
  the n-type material
** Reverse Bias Reigon:
-> a condition in a semiconductor device where it does not conduct current

** Ideal diode:
-> No current flow until we get above zero volts
-> Infinite resistance until theirs come current flow
-> once flow starts the resistance is zero
